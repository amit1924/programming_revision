DAX Aggregate Functions

DAX (Data Analysis eXpressions) is the formula language of Power Pivot. The aggregate functions — SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, DISTINCTCOUNT, MEDIAN and the iterator X-versions — collapse thousands of rows into a single answer, live inside measures.

What Are DAX Aggregate Functions?

One number out of many rows — the heartbeat of every measure.

BIG PICTURE
KindWhat it doesExamples
Column aggregatesScan one column, skip blanks, return a single valueSUM · AVERAGE · MIN · MAX · MEDIAN · COUNT
Counting & distinctCount rows or unique valuesCOUNTROWS · DISTINCTCOUNT · COUNTA
Iterators (X-functions)Calculate per row FIRST, then aggregate the resultsSUMX · AVERAGEX · MINX · MAXX · COUNTX
FilteredAggregate under a modified filterCALCULATE(SUM(…), filter)

Excel déjà vu: SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX and COUNT work almost like their Excel twins. The difference — DAX always gets a column or table, and the answer respects filter context (pivot rows/slicers) automatically.

Total Salary := SUM(Jobs[Salary]) → 1,655,000

The Counting Family — COUNT · COUNTA · COUNTROWS · DISTINCTCOUNT

Four ways to count, four different answers. Pick the one that matches your question.

1

Distinct titles on the job list

"Data Analyst" appears 3 times — DISTINCTCOUNT counts it once. 5 unique titles out of 8 rows.

Distinct Titles := DISTINCTCOUNT(Jobs[Job Title]) → 5

COUNT vs COUNTA: if a column can hold TRUE/FALSE, plain COUNT quietly skips those rows. Use COUNTA when the data type isn't guaranteed.

SUM vs SUMX — Pick the Right One

Same result for plain columns. Different result the moment a per-row calculation is involved.

CaseSUM(Jobs[Salary])SUMX(Jobs, …)
Column already exists1,655,000 ✓1,655,000 ✓
Need salary × 0.10 first❌ No column for it165,500 ✓

Rule of thumb: need a plain column total? SUM. Need to compute something per row before adding (bonus, currency conversion, tax)? SUMX. Same logic applies to the whole X family.

Euro Total := SUMX(Jobs, Jobs[Salary] * 0.85) ← convert every salary, then sum

CALCULATE — Aggregates Under a Filter

The most powerful function in DAX: run an aggregate in a modified filter context.

KING OF DAX
CALCULATE(expression, filter1, filter2, …)
1

"Total US salary only"

The aggregate inside runs as if only the United States rows existed.

US Salary := CALCULATE(SUM(Jobs[Salary]), Jobs[Country] = "United States") → 1,385,000
CountryRowsUS Salary
United States41,385,000
India2BLANK
United Kingdom1BLANK

The magic: in a pivot, CALCULATE sees the row's own context (India row → India) and your new filter. The new filter can override the row — which is exactly how "US salary per country" and "% of total" work.

2

% of grand total — remove all filters

REMOVEFILTERS clears the pivot's row filters so the denominator is the whole table.

Salary % of Total := DIVIDE(SUM(Jobs[Salary]), CALCULATE(SUM(Jobs[Salary]), REMOVEFILTERS(Jobs)))
CountrySalary% of Total
United States1,385,00083.7%
India210,00012.7%

Row Context vs Filter Context

The two "worlds" a DAX formula can live in — everything clicks once you see them.

Row ContextFilter Context
SeesOne row at a timeMany rows, filtered down
Created byIterators (SUMX…) and calculated columnsPivot rows, columns, slicers, CALCULATE
Typical questionWhat is THIS row's value?What's the total of ALL visible rows?
ToolJobs[Salary] * 0.10SUM(Jobs[Salary])
1

An iterator lives in row context… but needs the aggregate

Inside SUMX you're in row context. To summarize the whole table from there you must convert context with CALCULATE — called context transition.

:= SUMX(Jobs, CALCULATE( SUM( Jobs[Salary] ) ))

Keep it simple: use column aggregates in measures (filter context), use X-functions when a per-row step is unavoidable, and let CALCULATE bridge the two.

The DISTINCTCOUNT Trap — Totals Don't Add Up

Distinct counts are not additive. The grand total is almost never the sum of the subtotals.

1

Distinct companies per country

Same company hires in two countries → counted once in each country, but only once in the grand total.

CountryDistinct companies
United States3
India2
Sum of rows5 (looks right)
Actual grand total4 (one company in both US + India)

Expected behaviour, not a bug. A value that appears in two categories is counted once per category, but only once overall. If you need totals that add up, use COUNT or COUNTROWS instead — just know they count rows, not unique values.

How Blanks Are Handled

Each aggregate treats an empty cell its own way — know it before you trust the number.

SUM(Jobs[Salary])Skips the blank330,000

The silent trap: SUM and AVERAGE ignore blanks — so a column full of missing salaries quietly returns 0 instead of telling you it's broken. Guard it: =IF(COUNTROWS(Jobs) = COUNT(Jobs[Salary]), SUM(Jobs[Salary]), BLANK())
