Excel — Relationship Cardinality (1:1 · 1:N · N:N)
Step by step
The "how many" of a relationship — the 1 and the ★ on the diagram line

One-to-Many · Many-to-One · Many-to-Many

Cardinality describes how many rows of one table connect to rows of another. It decides how Excel filters and sums across tables in your data model — and whether your pivot table gives you correct answers or double-counted chaos.

"one" side "many" side fact / result
1

What Is Cardinality?

"Cardinal" = how many. It's the count of matching rows on each side of a relationship.

THE CONCEPT
CardinalityNotationOne job has…Real-life sound
One-to-One1 : 1exactly 1 detail rowEach employee has ONE passport
One-to-Many1 : Nmany skill rowsOne job → many skills
Many-to-OneN : 1—Many jobs → one company
Many-to-ManyN : Nmany on both sidesStudents ↔ Courses

Easy read: read a diagram line from left to right. "1 : N" means one row on the left links to many rows on the right.

"N : 1" vs "1 : N"

They're the SAME relationship, just described from opposite directions. In Excel/Power Pivot you always draw it as dim (1) → fact (many).

Companies (1) ⟷ jobs (many) = "many jobs to one company" = N : 1. Same line, different words.

Many-to-Many (N : N) — Handle With Care

Many rows in A match many rows in B — and back. It needs a BRIDGE table to stay healthy.

DANGEROUS
1

A student takes many courses; a course has many students.

4

The warning

Excel can model N:N with bridge tables, but a raw N:N link (both sides ★) causes double counting and slow models.

Rule: never draw a direct N:N line in Power Pivot. Always split it with a bridge table into two 1:N relationships.

6

Reading the Diagram View Symbols

How Power Pivot draws cardinality on the relationship line.

SymbolOn the lineMeaning
1On the dimension sideUnique key — each row matches once
★On the fact sideRepeated key — each row matches many times
1 ⟷ ★Whole lineOne-to-many (the healthy default)
★ ⟷ ★Whole lineMany-to-many (trouble — bridge it)

Check this every time: open Diagram View and confirm the 1 sits on the dimension (unique) side and the ★ on the fact (repeated) side. If both sides show ★, your model has a problem.

The same 1:N job→skills model, and what correct vs wrong counting looks like.

Wrong — filtering against the arrow

Counting job_id from the WRONG side of the arrow gives the same total everywhere.

job_title_shortCount of job_id
Data Analyst32672
Data Engineer32672
Data Scientist32672

Same number in every row = classic cardinality/filter-direction bug. The arrow flows one way; the measure must sit on the side that respects it.

3

Memory hook

Dimensions "1" → filter. Facts "★" → count. Let the 1 side drive the filter, the ★ side hold the values.

Filter from 1, measure on ★. Break that and you'll see repeated totals.
