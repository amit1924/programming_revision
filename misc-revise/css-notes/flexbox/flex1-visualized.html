Key insight: flex-basis: 0 is what makes items start from zero before dividing space. Without it (flex-basis: auto), items keep their content width and flex-grow only distributes the remaining space after content. Using flex: 1 (1 1 0) means all space is divided equally from scratch.

When items have different flex-grow values: space is distributed proportionally. If A has flex-grow: 2 and B,C have 1, A gets 2/4 of the space (50%), B and C get 1/4 each (25%).

6. Common Misconceptions — What flex: 1 does NOT do

✕ flex: 1 sets width to 100%

No. It sets flex-basis to 0 and lets grow divide space. Items share the row, not stack.

✕ flex: 1 works without a container

No. The parent must have display: flex. Otherwise, flex properties are ignored.

✕ flex: 1 makes items equal height

That's align-items: stretch (the default). flex: 1 controls width, not height.

✕ flex: 1 means "take all space"

It takes equal share of space alongside siblings. If only one item has flex: 1, it fills the rest.

Remember this one rule: If you want an item to fill available space, give it flex: 1 (which is flex-grow: 1; flex-shrink: 1; flex-basis: 0). If you want multiple items to share space equally, give all of them flex: 1.
